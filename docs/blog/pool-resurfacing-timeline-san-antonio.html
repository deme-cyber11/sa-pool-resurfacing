How Long Does Pool Resurfacing Take in San Antonio? (2026)

Updated May 2026 · 5 min read · Alamo Pool Resurfacing

Most San Antonio homeowners ask this at the start of every estimate: "How long will my pool be out of commission?" It's a fair question — especially if you have kids at home or entertain regularly during Texas summers.

The short answer: 5–8 days from the time we drain to the time the pool is ready to swim. The long answer depends on what we find when we drain it. Here's the full breakdown.

Quick summary: Drain + prep (1–2 days) → Surface application (1 day) → Fill (1 day) → Startup chemistry (3–5 days) → Swimming. Total: 6–9 days in most cases.

Day-by-Day Pool Resurfacing Timeline

Day 1

Drain and Initial Inspection

We pump out the pool (typically 8–12 hours depending on size) and do our actual condition assessment once it's fully drained. This is when we confirm whether any structural repairs are needed — cracks, delamination, exposed rebar — that weren't visible with water in the pool. If repairs are needed, this adds 1–2 days to the timeline.

Day 2

Surface Prep

We acid wash or sandblast the existing surface to remove scale, contaminants, and any loose material that would prevent the new surface from bonding properly. Any structural repairs are done now. Proper prep is the single biggest factor in how long the new surface lasts — shortcuts here are how you end up with peeling and delamination problems within 3–5 years.

Day 3

Surface Application

The actual resurfacing — plaster, quartz aggregate, or pebble — is applied in a single day. Timing within this day matters: if we finish application in the morning, we can start the fill the same evening. Weather has to be cooperating — application can't happen in rain or high winds. San Antonio's summer storm pattern occasionally forces a 1-day reschedule.

Days 4–5

Fill

Refilling typically takes 24–48 hours depending on your pool size and water pressure. We prefer to keep a hose running continuously rather than stopping and starting — partial fill marks can appear if the water level sits at the same point for more than a few hours on fresh plaster or quartz.

Days 5–9

Startup Chemistry (Critical)

This is the phase most contractors don't explain well — but it's arguably the most important. The first 7–10 days after filling establish the chemical profile that determines how your surface cures and how long it lasts. We brush the pool daily (pulling plaster dust off the surface), balance pH and calcium hardness incrementally, and test every 24 hours. You can typically swim after 7–10 days from fill completion.

What Can Slow Things Down?

Structural damage discovered at drain: Significant cracks, delamination, or exposed rebar add 1–3 days to repair. We always include a contingency discussion in our estimates — but we don't know for certain until we drain.
  • Rain during application: Fresh plaster and quartz surfaces can't get rained on within the first 24 hours. Summer afternoon storms are San Antonio's biggest scheduling variable — we monitor forecasts and reschedule application day if there's significant storm risk.
  • High winds: Debris contamination in fresh plaster is a real concern. We'll postpone application if gusts are 20+ mph.
  • Scheduling backlog: May through August is our busiest season. Book 3–4 weeks out during summer — don't wait until your pool is completely failed before calling.
  • Permit requirements: Most resurfacing doesn't require a permit in San Antonio, but structural repairs sometimes do. We'll flag this if applicable.

San Antonio Timing Considerations

The best time to schedule pool resurfacing in San Antonio is February through April. The weather is mild, contractors have more scheduling flexibility, and you'll be swimming again before the real heat hits in June.

If you're scheduling in summer, expect project timing to look the same but with a 3–4 week lead time to get on the schedule. The work itself goes the same — startup chemistry may move slightly faster in summer heat, which actually helps cure time.

How to Prepare for Your Pool Resurfacing

  • Clear deck furniture and equipment within 6 feet of the pool edge
  • Mark your utility lines if you have buried irrigation or lighting near the pool
  • Notify neighbors if you share a wall — equipment runs during business hours
  • Arrange an alternative for kids/pets during the drain + application days
  • Plan your water source — filling a pool uses 10,000–25,000 gallons depending on size
